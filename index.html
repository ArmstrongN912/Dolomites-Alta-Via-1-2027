The four checks

1 · Dates

Hike 4–9 Aug, arrive by 3 Aug

Six walking days, Wednesday to Monday. Sleep at Lago di Braies on Tue 3 Aug so you start walking at 8am. Fly home on Tue 10 Aug. Arriving on 2 Aug leaves a spare day for delays and jet lag.

2 · DIY?

Yes, no guide needed

AV1 is waymarked the whole way with a "1" in a blue triangle plus red-and-white CAI paint and numbered signposts. There's no via ferrata on the main line. A few short cabled steps and one steep scree pass (Forcella del Lago) have easier alternatives, given below. It's mapped in OpenStreetMap, so Komoot routes it on the real paths.

3 · Huts

All open in August, but book now

All the planned huts are open in early August, and every one has a backup. Summer 2027 booking is already open, and some huts were nearly full for August by October 2026. Lagazuoi and Croda da Lago are the two to lock in first.

4 · Airports

Venice in & out (Singapore: Munich in)

China: nonstop flights to Venice from Shanghai (China Eastern) and Beijing (Air China). Singapore: there's no nonstop to Venice, so fly SQ nonstop to Munich and take the train to Dobbiaco, then fly home from Venice with one stop. Everyone meets at Dobbiaco on 3 Aug.

What changed from the Bookatrekking 6-day version: (1) Its day 2 (Pederü → Lagazuoi, 17.5 km, +1,540 m, ~7h45) is split across two days with a night in the Fanes meadows. (2) The night near Cinque Torri becomes Croda da Lago, so the descent from Nuvolau goes by the plain path to Passo Giau, avoiding the ladders and cables of the Ra Gusela route. (3) It finishes at Listolade (bus stop) instead of the longer 1,650 m descent to Agordo via Carestiato. Same famous scenery, and the longest day drops from 7h45 to about 6h30.

Accommodation, checked

Each night has a first choice and a fallback on the same line. Opening seasons are from the 2026 season (2027 dates are usually similar but not yet confirmed). Expect half board of roughly €55–90 per person in dorms, more for private rooms. Bring a sleeping-bag liner; towels are often not provided.

Phone numbers are from Google Maps listings; email or each hut's own booking page usually works better than phone. Several huts charge for showers and bottled water (Lagazuoi, Biella and Staulanza are known for it), so bring cash.

Getting there and back

From China → Venice (VCE)

  • Shanghai → Venice nonstop: China Eastern, about 5 flights a week as of May 2026. It's an overnight flight landing in the morning, ideal for heading straight to the mountains.
  • Beijing → Venice nonstop: Air China, 4 a week since July 2026. It lands in the evening, so sleep in Mestre and travel up the next morning; that means landing by 2 Aug.
  • Backup: Milan Malpensa has daily nonstops from Beijing and Shanghai, then about 3 hours by train to Venice.
  • Home on 10 Aug: Venice → Shanghai or Beijing on the same airlines. Check which weekdays they fly in summer 2027 once schedules are published.

From Singapore → Munich in, Venice out

  • In: Singapore Airlines flies nonstop daily to Munich, arriving early morning (SQ328 lands 06:40 on the current schedule). Train Munich → Fortezza → Dobbiaco is about 5–5.5 h, so you can reach Braies the same afternoon.
  • Out: there's no nonstop Venice–Singapore, so book a one-stop ticket via a European or Gulf hub. The alternative is the SQ nonstop from Milan Malpensa (12:35 departure), but that means a very early train from Venice.
  • Why not Munich both ways? The trail ends in the south near Belluno, about 2–3 h from Venice but 6+ h from Munich.

Doing it without a guide

Waymarking

Follow the blue triangle with a "1", plus red-white paint flashes with CAI path numbers (each day card lists them). Signposts at every junction give times to the next hut. Most junctions are clear; in fog, check the app at each fork.

Komoot setup

In the Komoot planner, set the day's start and end, then add the "via" points listed on each card (use "Copy waypoints"). Komoot snaps to OSM paths tagged as Alta Via 1. Download each day for offline use, and check the line matches the named passes.

Backup navigation

Carry a second offline app on another phone (e.g. Mapy or OsmAnd), a power bank and a paper map: the Cicerone AV1 guide's 1:25,000 map booklet, or Tabacco 1:25,000 sheets bought in Cortina or at the huts.

Weather & safety

August afternoons often bring thunderstorms, so start by 7:30 and be over the high passes by early afternoon. The emergency number is 112. Tell each hut your next stop; staff phone ahead for you. Bring poles for days 3, 4 and 6.

The only spots that need care: short cabled zigzags below Forcella Sora Forno (Day 1); the steep, loose scree descent from Forcella del Lago (Day 3, with an easier variant); the optional WWI tunnels down from Lagazuoi (headlamp, steep and wet, Day 4); and the Ra Gusela route down from Nuvolau, which this plan avoids. For a group that has done the Tour du Mont Blanc, none of this needs a guide.
